01 Luberon

The Villages

Stone villages perched on cliff edges, ochre-red facades glowing in the afternoon light, platane-shaded squares where pétanque and rosé are the only agenda. These are the hilltop jewels of the Luberon.

THE PICK — MARKET DAY

Gordes

The Crown Jewel

The most photographed village in Provence. Stone houses cascade down a cliff face in a gravity-defying tumble of warm limestone. The classic view from the D15 approach road is the shot that sells a thousand plane tickets.

Market — Tuesday

Roussillon

The Ochre Village

Buildings painted in every shade of red, orange, and gold, drawn from the ochre cliffs that cradle it. Walk the Sentier des Ocres — a 30-minute trail through surreal formations that look like they belong on Mars.

Market — Thursday

Bonnieux

The Literary Village

Perched on a ridge with sweeping views toward the Luberon massif. Home to two Michelin-starred restaurants, a legendary bakery, and the kind of quiet confidence that comes from centuries of knowing you’re beautiful.

Market — Friday

Lourmarin

The Chic Village

The “Saint-Germain of the Luberon.” Galleries, boutiques, and the liveliest café scene in the valley. Albert Camus is buried here. The Friday market is one of the best in the region — come hungry.

Market — Friday

L’Isle-sur-la-Sorgue

The Venice of Provence

Waterwheels turn over crystal-clear canals that lace through the old town. The Sunday antique market draws 300+ dealers and is one of the largest in Europe. Even on quiet days, the green water is hypnotic.

Market — Sunday & Thursday

Ménerbes

Peter Mayle’s Village

Made famous by A Year in Provence, Ménerbes remains authentically unhurried. The hilltop views over vineyards at sunset are among the finest in the Luberon. Don’t miss the corkscrew museum at nearby Domaine de la Citadelle.

No fixed market — just magic

“In Provence, time moves differently. The light is golden longer. The wine tastes better because you’re drinking it in the place where the grapes grew.”

04 Les Vignobles

The Wine Trail

Luberon rosés pale as dawn. Organic reds from the château Russell Crowe made famous. Formal gardens with classified status. Corkscrew museums. The Luberon wine scene is serious but never solemn.

Château La Canorgue

Bonnieux  ·  15 min from Andéols

Organic pioneer of the Luberon, featured in A Good Year. Family-run, award-winning rosé and whites. Relaxed, authentic, no pretension.

🌿 Organic

Château Val Joanis

Pertuis  ·  30 min from Andéols

Stunning estate with a Jardin Remarquable. Wine tasting in the cellar, then stroll through formal gardens with a glass of rosé. Group-friendly and photogenic.

Jardin Remarquable

Château La Verrerie

Puget-sur-Durance  ·  25 min

Historic 18th-century estate. Three tasting parcours available. Ancient stone buildings amid rolling vineyards — the setting alone is worth the visit.

Three Parcours

Domaine de la Citadelle

Ménerbes  ·  20 min

Excellent Luberon wines plus a museum of 1,200 corkscrews. Fun, quirky, and the terrace views are spectacular. Culture and wine in one stop.

Corkscrew Museum
05 La Méditerranée

The Coast

Dramatic limestone fjords plunging into turquoise water. France’s answer to the Amalfi Coast, but wilder. Cassis has its own AOC wine appellation and the highest maritime cliff in Europe next door.

Cassis

One of the most beautiful small ports in the Mediterranean. Pastel buildings, fishing boats, cliff-backed beaches, and its own AOC wine appellation famous for whites and rosés. Evening aperitif on the port watching the boats come in is non-negotiable.

Calanque d’En-Vau

The most spectacular of the Calanques — a narrow fjord between towering white limestone cliffs, with water so turquoise it looks rendered. Hike in or boat in from Cassis port. Book the morning slot before the Mistral kicks up.

Cap Canaille

The highest maritime cliff in Europe at 394 metres. Drive the Route des Crêtes between Cassis and La Ciotat, pull over at every viewpoint. The sunset from up here, looking down at the Calanques, is the photo of a lifetime.

07 À Ne Pas Manquer

Don’t Miss

The moments that become the trip. Lavender in peak bloom. Ochre canyons on another planet. A 12th-century abbey where monks still live and work. Set the alarm. Make the drive. Thank us later.

Lavender & History
ONCE IN A LIFETIME

Abbaye Notre-Dame de Sénanque

The most photographed lavender field in the world — perfect rows of purple with a 12th-century Cistercian abbey behind. The monks still live and work here. Late June is peak bloom.

Tip: Arrive at sunrise (~6 AM) for the light and no crowds. The scent of lavender in the morning heat is transcendent. 30 min from Andéols via Gordes.
Lavender Fields

Valensole Plateau

800 km² of lavender fields rolling to the horizon. The most dramatic lavender landscape in Provence. The lone tree, the stone barn, the crossroads — the photography spots are legendary.

Tip: Best at golden hour. ~1h 15m from Andéols. Combine with a visit to the clifftop village of Moustiers-Sainte-Marie for the full day trip.
Natural Wonder

Sentier des Ocres

A 30–45 minute walk through surreal ochre cliff formations in shades of red, orange, and gold. It looks like Mars, it feels like a dream, and it costs €3. Adjacent to Roussillon village.

Tip: Wear shoes you don’t mind staining ochre-red. The pigment is real and it gets everywhere. That’s part of the charm. 15 min from Andéols.
08 The Insider Tip

Skip the Autoroute. Take the D-Roads.

The D-roads — the départementales — are the soul of Provence. Winding through vineyards, past lavender fields, under canopies of plane trees. They’re slower. That’s the point. Every wrong turn reveals a village you didn’t know existed, a view you can’t find on Google, a roadside stand selling melons and honey. The A7 gets you there. The D2, D36, D943 — they make you fall in love.

“The friends are closer because you laughed harder. And when the lavender blooms and the sun sets over a hilltop village and someone you love is beside you — you’ll remember this week for the rest of your life.”

Insider Tips
  • French autoroutes are toll roads — budget €15-25 per car per direction.
  • Fête de la Musique tip: no glass containers, comfortable shoes. Avignon has multiple stages across the old town.
  • Luberon market schedule is everything: Sun = L'Isle-sur-la-Sorgue, Tue = GORDES (the unmissable one), Thu = Roussillon.
  • Chase Sapphire Reserve provides primary CDW coverage internationally for rentals.
  • 10 people = book 2 groups of 5 for flights (better availability).
What to Skip
  • Pont du Gard on a Saturday — coach tour chaos. Go early on a weekday or skip entirely.
  • Lavender 'museums' and 'experiences' in Sault — overpriced tourist traps. Just drive through the fields.
  • Aix-en-Provence Cours Mirabeau restaurants — beautiful street, tourist-priced food. Walk one block north for the real stuff.
