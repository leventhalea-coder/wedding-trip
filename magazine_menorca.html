Menorca The Decompression

Not Ibiza. Not Mallorca. A UNESCO Biosphere Reserve where fishing boats still deliver to restaurant kitchens, where the beaches are Caribbean-turquoise but the pace is Mediterranean-slow, and where the biggest decision of the day is which cala to discover.

Season June · Pre-Peak
Duration 4–5 Days
Character Beaches · Lobster · Slow
Water 20–24°C · Crystal

"The island is 47 kilometers long. You can drive end to end in forty-five minutes. But you won't — because every cove you pass will make you pull over, climb down, and swim."

01 · The Calas

The Beaches

Menorca has over a hundred beaches and coves. The south coast delivers postcard turquoise and white sand; the north is wilder, redder, less crowded. These are the seven that matter most — with satellite views so you can see exactly what you're walking into.

01
THE PICK — BEACH

Cala Mitjana

Best All-Rounder

The jewel. A sheltered cove framed by pine-covered cliffs with impossibly clear water. Ten-minute walk through fragrant pines from the car park. Her tiny sister, Cala Mitjaneta, hides around the rocks — even more secluded.

15 min from Torralbenc Excellent Snorkeling No Facilities
02

Cala Macarella & Macarelleta

The Icons

Menorca's most famous beaches — and earned. Macarella is a half-moon of sugar-white sand sloping into gin-clear water, backed by dramatic cliffs. Macarelleta, a short scramble around the rocks, is smaller, wilder, and often clothing-optional. Go early morning to beat the crowds.

20 min + 15 min walk Very Good Snorkeling Small Beach Bar
03

Cala Turqueta

The Quieter Stunner

Like Macarella's less-discovered cousin. Pine forest, white sand, transparent water. The name says it all — turquoise. Quieter than its famous neighbor, especially in June before the August crush arrives.

25 min + 15 min walk Good Snorkeling No Facilities
04
THE PICK — WILD BEACH

Cala Pregonda

The Wild Card

Menorca's most spectacular wild beach. Red-gold sand, rust-colored rocks, islets offshore — it looks like Mars meets the Mediterranean. Requires a thirty-minute hike from Binimel·là. Worth every step. Bring everything — there is absolutely nothing here except beauty.

35 min + 30 min hike Outstanding Snorkeling North Coast · Wild
05

Binibeca

The Southeast Gem

Hypnotic gradations of blue water on the southeast coast. The whitewashed fishing village of Binibeca Vell nearby is worth a wander — and Bucaneros beach bar is the island's benchmark for feet-in-sand seafood.

25 min from Torralbenc Excellent off rocks Beach Bar
06

Santo Tomás & Binigaus

The Coastal Walk

Santo Tomás is the nearest proper beach to Torralbenc — fine sand, shallow water, easy access. Walk west along the coast twenty minutes to reach Binigaus: a wild, undeveloped beach backed by pine forest. Binigaus is the hidden gem.

15 min from Torralbenc Easy Access 20 min walk to Binigaus
07

Cala Algaiarens

La Vall · Twin Bays

Twin horseshoe bays on the northwest coast. Shallow, sheltered water backed by pine forest. Turtles splash around here. Ten-minute walk from the car park. If you make it to the north coast, this is the gentle complement to Pregonda's drama.

40 min + 10 min walk Good Snorkeling Northwest · Quiet

02 · Where to Stay

Three Hotels,
Three Temperaments

A vineyard farmstead for countryside romance, a French-cool finca for design-forward retreat, and a 16th-century palace for walking to dinner every night.

★ The Recommendation

Hotel Torralbenc

The One

A restored 19th-century farmstead across seventy acres of vineyards and woodland on the south coast. Only 27 rooms. Saltwater infinity pool overlooking vineyards rolling to the sea. A working winery. A Basque chef running the kitchen. Candlelit private cave dinners for two. Condé Nast Traveller calls it one of Europe's best luxury farm stays.

€350–600 / night · Alaior, South Coast · Small Luxury Hotels
The Alternative

Menorca Experimental

The Cool Kid

The Experimental Group brought their signature French-cool aesthetic to a converted 19th-century finca on thirty hectares of farmland. Interiors by Dorothée Meilichzon — soft pinks, signature arch headboards. Nine freestanding villas for maximum privacy. The bar is the real star; this group's original expertise is nightlife and dining.

€400–700 / night · Alaior · Michelin Guide Recognized
The Town Option

Faustino Gran

Relais & Châteaux · The Palace

A 16th-century palace in Ciutadella's old town, meticulously restored into a Relais & Châteaux property. Soaring three-story ceilings, antique shuttered windows, olive-tree courtyards. Steps from the port, the cathedral, and the best restaurants on the island's western half. Walk to dinner. Walk home.

€350–650 / night · Ciutadella Old Town · Relais & Châteaux

"Lobster season runs spring through late summer. June catches caldereta de langosta at its absolute prime."

03 · Where to Eat

The Table

From a harbor-front lobster institution to a former shoe factory turned fine dining, from clifftop seafood to a raw bar with a cult following. Menorca eats seriously.

The Essential Meal
THE PICK — ESSENTIAL MEAL

Café Balear

Three Generations · Their Own Fishing Boat

On the port of Ciutadella. The Rosa Santa Primera delivers daily to this kitchen. This is THE caldereta de langosta of Menorca — the version all others are measured against. Also iconic: lobster with fried eggs and chips. Book the interior; the harbor terrace is walk-in only. Arrive with patience and a cold drink.

The order: Caldereta de langosta (for two, ~€75pp). Then: lobster with fried eggs and chips.

Ciutadella Port · Caldereta ~€75/pp
Fine Dining
THE PICK — FINE DINING

Smoix

The Pre-Wedding Splurge

Former shoe factory, now Menorca's most inventive kitchen. Crawfish ravioli. Beluga lentils with king-prawn tempura. Whitewashed patio, exposed rafters, industrial-romantic. This is your celebration dinner.

The order: Crawfish ravioli, beluga lentils with king-prawn tempura. Book ahead.

Lobster Village

Sa Llagosta

Fornells · Ground Zero for Caldereta

Fine-dining-meets-tavern in the whitewashed fishing village where caldereta was born. Outstanding lobster rice too. Try the scorpionfish with shiitake. Follow with ice cream at Ricardo Riera down the harbor.

The order: Caldereta, plus the scorpionfish with shiitake. Outstanding lobster rice too.

The Art Experience

Cantina at Hauser & Wirth

Illa del Rei · King's Island

A decommissioned 18th-century naval hospital on its own island, now housing a world-class art gallery. Only accessible by fifteen-minute ferry from Mahón harbor. Dinner is set amid an olive grove strung with lights. Tour the gallery before. Reservations include the ferry.

Mahón Harbor · Ferry Included
Creative Tapas

Pez Limón

Best Croquetas on the Island

Rattan-lit alley in Ciutadella. Creative Mediterranean tapas — the family-recipe croquetas are famous. Natural wine list the staff actually love talking about. The cheesecake has a following.

Raw Bar

Oysters Menorca

The Cult Spot · Mahón

Small, cool raw bar. Rotating European oysters, caviar, anchovy pasta with a cult following. Champagne by the glass. Reserve or arrive the moment they unlock the door.

Beach Bar

Bucaneros

Binibeca Beach · The Benchmark

Whitewashed fisherman's hut with a red door, wooden tables on the rocks, straw umbrellas. Fish off the rocks between courses. The island's benchmark beach bar. Walk-in; arrive early.

The order: Whatever fish came in. Walk-in, arrive early.

Vineyard Dinner

Bodegas Binifadet

Under the Stars · String Lights

A pioneering Menorcan winery with a restaurant on a vine-shaded terrace. Sobrasada platters, chargrilled vegetable rice, pork cheeks with Mahón cheese. More special at night when the string lights come on.

The order: Sobrasada platter, chargrilled vegetable rice, estate wine. Better at night under string lights.

04 · The Coffee Trail

An Honest
Caffeine Report

Menorca's specialty coffee scene is small but real — concentrated in Ciutadella with a couple of outposts in Mahón. Don't expect Melbourne. But you can get a proper flat white. Here are the two that anchor each town.

Ciutadella

La Reina Specialty Coffee

Best Flat White on the Island

Right on Plaça des Born. Consistently the best-pulled espresso in Ciutadella. Flat white €3.50. Large terrace for morning people-watching. Also open evenings 5–11pm for after-beach caffeine.

Mahón

Pigalle

French Bakery · Mistral Beans from Mallorca

French bakery in Mahón's colorful historic center. Specialty beans from Mistral, a roaster in Mallorca. The croissants and pain au chocolat are the real deal. Grab their payés sourdough for the beach.

Better Coffee

Ciutadella · Tiny espresso bar rotating different beans daily. Proper specialty. Flat white €3.50.

Catedral Coffee

Ciutadella · Opens 7am — your jet-lag savior. Good flat whites, iced coffees, açaí bowls.

Pipet & Co

Mahón · Best breakfast in town. Perfectly poached eggs, avocado, solid coffee. On Plaça Bastió.

Your mornings at Torralbenc start with their included breakfast — Mahón cheese, local produce, estate quality. On days in Ciutadella, hit La Reina. Mahón days: Pigalle. Don't stress about coffee on beach days.

05 · Golden Hour

Where to Watch
the Sun Go Down

On an island this size, the west coast sunset spots feel like ritual. Three venues, three temperatures — from clifftop cocktails in a cave to a lighthouse dinner to a raw stone arch over open water.

◐

Artrutx Sea Club

Best Sunset Dinner on the Island

Directly beneath the Cap d'Artrutx lighthouse, built 1858. The terrace faces due west over open water — arguably the best sunset seat on Menorca. Come an hour before sunset for cocktails on the Balinese beds. Stay through dinner. Mediterranean sharing plates, seafood rices. You're here for the theater.

◑

Cova d'en Xoroi

A Bar Carved into the Cliff

A famous bar literally carved into the clifftop at Cala en Porter. Multiple cave terraces open to the sea at different heights. Sunset cocktails from inside the rock. Becomes a club later — but at golden hour, it's all atmosphere and vertigo.

◓

Pont d'en Gil

The Natural Stone Arch · Walk the Camí

A dramatic natural stone arch over the sea on the west coast near Ciutadella. No bar, no restaurant — just you, the rock, and the light. Walk here via the Camí de Cavalls coastal path. The arch frames the sunset like a cathedral window. Bring a bottle of something cold.

07
The Insider Tip

The Drink You Cannot
Leave Without Having

Pomada. Gin Xoriguer — distilled in Mahón's port since the 18th-century British occupation — mixed with cloudy lemonade over ice. It's dangerously easy to drink. Order one at every sunset. Buy a distinctive green-handled bottle at the distillery to take home. The recipe is older than the country that occupies the island.

Gin Xoriguer Distillery

Visit the working distillery on Mahón's port. Taste the gin straight, then as pomada. Buy the bottle with the green handle — it's the only souvenir that matters. The distillery also makes herbes and a palo liqueur worth trying.

Insider Tips
  • Menorca Experimental is inland — don't expect ocean views from the hotel. The beaches are 10-15 min by car.
  • Rent an automatic transmission car via Chase UR points (26K) at the airport. You'll need it — no Ubers here.
  • Caldereta is a two-person minimum and runs ~€75+ per person for the lobster. Budget accordingly.
  • June is early season — beaches are quieter, but some beach bars and restaurants don't open until mid-June. Check before driving.
What to Skip
  • Fornells harbor-front tourist restaurants with giant menus in 6 languages — stick to Sa Llagosta and Es Cranc for the real caldereta.
  • Cala Galdana — once beautiful, now overdeveloped with high-rise hotels. Go to Mitjana instead (15 min walk from Galdana's parking).
  • Son Bou mega-beach — largest on the island but feels like Benidorm. Not the Menorca experience.
